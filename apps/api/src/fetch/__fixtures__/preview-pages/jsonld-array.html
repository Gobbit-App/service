Starting a sourdough culture takes about a week of patient feeding. Mix equal weights of whole wheat flour and water in a clean jar, cover it loosely and leave it somewhere warm. Each day, discard half and feed it again with fresh flour and water, and watch for bubbles and a mild, tangy smell.

Once the starter doubles reliably within six hours of a feed, it is ready to bake with. Mix the flour and most of the water first and let it rest for an hour so the flour hydrates fully, then add the starter, the salt and the last of the water, squeezing everything together until the dough feels even.

Over the next few hours, give the dough a set of stretches and folds every half hour. It will turn smooth, airy and a little jiggly. Shape it into a tight round, place it seam side up in a floured basket and let it rest in the fridge overnight so the flavour develops slowly.

Bake in a covered pot that has been preheated for at least forty five minutes. Score the top with a sharp blade, bake covered for twenty minutes so the crust stays soft while the loaf rises, then uncover it and bake until the crust is deep brown. Let it cool fully before slicing.
